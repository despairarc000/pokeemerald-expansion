# Implicit lore (proposal)

The world has a past. The player is never told it. They see things and interpret them, the way Minecraft and similar worlds do. Explicit lore (text, characters who explain) is a later layer on top.

**Status:** a proposal by the AI for the owner to react to. Nothing here is decided. Background design, not a commitment.

**Core line served:** Not story locked (lore exists, is never forced).

## Rules for implicit lore

1. **Shown, not told.** Clues are things in the world: ruins, objects, scars on the land, how creatures behave, what people believe. No text box explains them.
2. **Direct predecessor.** Every clue has a physical cause in the world's history. A ruin is there because something happened. Nothing exists only to deliver a message.
3. **Never gates anything.** Noticing a clue never unlocks a required path. It only rewards curiosity.
4. **One hidden truth.** Authors keep a consistent "true history" (below). The game never confirms it. Players assemble their own reading, and more than one reading can fit.
5. **Beliefs are not facts.** Local folk tales differ between regions and sometimes contradict each other. They are what people believe, and they can be wrong.
6. **A few repeating motifs.** A small set of symbols and shapes appear across regions on shrines, graves, mines and machines, so players can sense a connection before they understand it.
7. **Later explicit lore must not contradict the hidden truth.** It may be unreliable (a biased account), but it may not overwrite what the world shows.
8. **Clues cost little.** Small, cheap things placed with care, not big scripted scenes. This fits the real RAM and ROM limits.

## The hidden truth (seed, for authors only)

The world was once shared by an ancient people (working name: the Keepers) who lived alongside Pokemon as neighbors, not as owners. Their home was the far continent, now Ur-Kalah. They tended the tides, the berries and the shrines, because the land and sea move in long cycles and the cycles decide who can live where.

The Keepers are gone. The record does not say why. The evidence allows several readings: a great flood, a slow leaving, or a choice to give something up. The world kept their traces and forgot their meaning.

The present civilizations grew on top of the leftovers without understanding them. Farmers inherit berry knowledge. Miners dig into old works. Neonport's corporations reverse-engineer old machinery for power, without asking what it was for. The tension is never stated: people use things whose purpose they do not know.

## What the player might see

| Where | What the world shows | What it hints, without saying |
|---|---|---|
| Veyra | Tide shrines that open and close with the sea | The Keepers built for the cycles |
| Duskhollow | Graves arranged for Pokemon as well as people | They mourned both the same way |
| Undervale | Fossils next to tools, not apart from them | Old cooperation, very old |
| Cragmoor | Mines that break into worked tunnels nobody dug | Someone was here before the miners |
| Neonport | Machine casings with worn symbols no one reads | Old tech under new tech |
| Skyreach | Floating islands anchored by ancient structures | Built to hold, not to impress |
| Sandara | Buried ruins that the dunes uncover and cover | The land hides and returns things |
| Meadowmere | A field boundary stone with a faint spiral, and a hedge where wild Pokemon gather at dusk | Even ordinary land remembers |
| Ur-Kalah | The source: huge, silent ruins, no explanation | The Keepers' home |

The repeating motif could be a spiral or ring. It is an example, not a decision.

## Why this fits the core

- **Not story locked:** nobody has to see any of it.
- **Doesn't revolve around the player:** the past happened without them, and the present people do not know it.
- **Explore free:** curiosity is the only key.
- **Fits rare finds:** a rare Pokemon appearing at an old shrine at the right tide is both a discovery and a clue.
